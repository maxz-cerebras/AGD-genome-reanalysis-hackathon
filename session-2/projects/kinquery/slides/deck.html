Challenge 8 — Find patients matching trial criteria

Which patients qualify, and why?

Team: Jakob & Max

AGD genome reanalysis hackathon — session 2synthetic registry data — not clinical evidence

The problem a study coordinator, the afternoon a protocol lands

The PI asks: "how many of our 100 registry families would qualify?" The criteria arrive as prose:

"female, born 1965–2005, with a first- or second-degree relative with breast cancer; exclude Li-Fraumeni"

  • The answer is not in the patient record. Family history lives in relatives and the pedigree; degree of relationship is stored nowhere.
  • Three things look like "has the disease": a gene finding, a MIM: molecular diagnosis, an ORDO: clinical diagnosis. Trials accept some, not others.
  • "Seizures" must match Generalized myoclonic seizure — an ontology question, not a string search.
  • 20 of 100 probands have no date of birth. An age criterion has to say what it did with them.
The registry: 100 probands, 529 relatives, one PhenoTips family export per line.
Data: session-2/data/phenopackets.jsonl — 100 families, HPO 2024-08-132 / 5

How we addressed it the model writes the question — code computes the answer

1 · Plain textCoordinator types the protocol criteria. Haiku or Sonnet translates into a typed query schema.
→
2 · GroundingHPO ids checked against HPO 2024-08-13; disease ids against the registry vocabulary. Corrections & unresolved terms shown, not hidden.
→
3 · Confirm & editCriteria as blocks in INCLUDE / EXCLUDE zones; add, remove, edit graphically or as JSON.
→
4 · Match & explainDeterministic matcher: every patient met / unmet / could not be assessed, with the exact fields that decided it.

Query categories

  • sex · birth year · age (at a reference date)
  • HPO phenotype — present / explicitly excluded / absent; descendants via the ontology
  • gene finding · diagnosis (molecular and/or clinical)
  • family history — degree, role, lineage, count, nested conditions
  • at-least-N-of · ALL · NOT; inclusion vs exclusion lists

What is computed, not stored

  • Relationship degree from pedigree.persons parent links: coefficient of relationship → 1st (parent, sibling, child), 2nd (grandparent, aunt, half-sibling), 3rd (first cousin); partners never count.
  • Three-valued logic: missing data propagates as could not be assessed, never as a silent exclusion.
  • Evidence: every claim is a JSON path in the file, e.g. relatives[3].phenotypicFeatures[0], plus the pedigree traversal.

Limits, and working with Claude

Limits (honest)

  • Cohort = 100 probands. Relatives are pedigree entries, not patient records.
  • Age from birth year only; most relatives have no DOB.
  • No variant pathogenicity, onset age or treatments in the export — "pathogenic BRCA1 variant" becomes "BRCA1 finding", flagged as an assumption.
  • Synthetic data; 100 families in memory. At scale the matcher needs an index; the pedigree maths does not change.

What Claude got wrong — and how we caught it

  • Hallucinated HPO ids with correct labels. HP:0010582 "Colonic polyposis" is actually Irregular epiphyses; Haiku's HP:0004395 "Colonic polyps" is Malnutrition. Caught by ontology grounding — the label now wins; unresolvable terms are shown with candidates instead of silently used.
  • "At least two of A, B, C" approximated as OR (12 matches instead of 3) until the schema could express it. The model itself flagged the shortcut in its assumptions list — that is how we noticed.
  • Tool output wrapped under a random key twice; validated against the schema, never trusted.
  • Solved the wrong problem first. Claude latched onto the pedigree — relationship maths, drawing, labels — and built a family-relationship tool. We redirected it: the user is a coordinator constructing a cohort; the pedigree is evidence, not the product. Inclusion/exclusion, "at least N of" and the funnel only came after that correction.
  • We verified the Claude-written degree maths on a hand-drawn family (half-aunt = 3rd, first cousin = 3rd, partner = unrelated) before trusting any count.
